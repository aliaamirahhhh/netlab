What I learned

Describe your design: why these routers, LANs and links, and how you chose the addresses.

My network consists of 4 routers, 4 LANs, and 4 links in between routers where each router acts as a gateway for its respective LAN. Each LAN also uses a different subnet to avoid address conflicts. The square topology allows redundancy within the network.

Which route summarises several LANs? Which networks does it cover, why is it correct, and what else would it attract if you added a network inside its range?

The summarized route is 10.80.32.0/23 which covers h2 and h3 LANs with an address of 10.80.32.0/24 and 10.80.33.0/24 respectively. This allows addresses within the range of 10.80.32.0 - 10.80.33.255 to be matched by a single route.

When you took a link down, what happened, and why couldn't your routes recover even though another path existed? What would have recovered?

Communication between some hosts failed because most static routes depended on that link since static routes do not automatically discover alternative paths or update whenever a link fails. A dynamic routing protocol such as OSPF would have detected the link failure and recalculate the best path to use.

In your network, what was the control plane? Who worked out the routes, and what does that mean for a network of 40 routers?

The control plane for this case was the static routing manually configured in network.yaml. Although it may be time-consuming and more prone to errors when managing 40 routers at once.
